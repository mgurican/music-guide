## TC Electronic BodyRez Analysis

The BodyRez is a highly specialized tool designed to correct the sterile, transient-heavy "quack" inherent to under-saddle piezo transducers. It achieves this by applying a pre-configured acoustic resonance EQ curve and targeted compression.

* **With the Majesty (Piezo Output):** The BodyRez **will** significantly improve the acoustic sound of your Majesty. When running the Majesty's piezo output through this pedal, it will restore the natural body and resonance of a mic'd acoustic guitar, smoothing out the harsh upper frequencies.
* **With the Majesty (Magnetic Pickups):** **Do not** run the magnetic signal (your humbuckers) through the BodyRez. The pedal's aggressive EQ filter will severely scoop your midrange and compress the signal, completely neutralizing the clarity of your high-end pickups.
* **With Other Guitars (Schecter, Epiphone):** Because these guitars lack piezo acoustic systems, the BodyRez serves no beneficial purpose for them. Running standard high-output humbuckers through acoustic enhancement filters will result in a muddy, buried tone.

## Signal Chain Optimization and Clipping Mechanisms

To hit the specific tonal targets of your favorite artists, you must utilize the distinct clipping mechanisms of your front-end drives correctly.

* **Ibanez TS808:** This circuit utilizes symmetrical clipping diodes. It applies a smooth, compressed overdrive while rolling off low-end frequencies and heavily boosting the mid-band (~720Hz). Use this strictly to tighten up the bass tracking on your 7-string or Majesty when playing intricate, Dream Theater-style rhythmic passages.
* **Boss SD-1:** This pedal utilizes asymmetrical clipping. It clips the top and bottom of the audio waveform unevenly, retaining higher-order harmonics and delivering a grittier, more amp-like breakup. This is your primary tool for pushing classic British voicings.
* **TC Spark Mini:** A clean +20dB linear boost. Place this at the end of your drive section. It pushes the V1 preamp tube of your amps harder into saturation without altering the fundamental EQ curve you have dialed in.

> **I wnat to learn some 1/2 step downtuned songs, and some dagdad tuned songs, which guitar should i use for downtuning?**

#### Gem Response
You are absolutely right to avoid alternating tunings on the Majesty. Adjusting a fully floating tremolo between E Standard, Eb Standard, and DADGAD throws the spring tension out of equilibrium, wrecking your bridge angle, action, and intonation. Keep the Majesty perfectly calibrated for E Standard.
Converting your Schecter Reaper into your dedicated downtuning workhorse is the most logical and practical solution.
Here is the technical breakdown of your plan to install a tremolo stopper (such as a Trem-ol-No or a brass block stopper) and run heavier strings on the Schecter:
**Pros of the Schecter Modification:**

* **Tuning Stability:** By installing a stopper and tightening the claw in the rear cavity, the tremolo block rests firmly against it. The bridge becomes "dive-only" (or fully fixed, depending on the hardware you choose). Changing to Eb or dropping to DADGAD will no longer throw the other strings out of tune.
* **Pickup Voicing:** The Petrucci Liquifire/Crunchlab set you installed in the Schecter is engineered precisely for lower tunings. The Crunchlab's ceramic bar magnet keeps the low-end incredibly tight and articulate when tracking dropped D or Eb power chords, which is essential for Tool or heavier Alice Cooper tracks.
* **Neck Profile:** The Reaper's neck radius and fretwork are highly conducive to the aggressive vibrato and bending you will use in lower tunings.

**Cons and Considerations:**

* **Loss of Upward Pitch:** You will completely lose the ability to pull up on the bar for flutters or upward pitch bends (unless you use a more complex locking system like the Trem-ol-No and frequently unlock it).
* **String Tension in DADGAD:** DADGAD requires dropping the 6th, 2nd, and 1st strings a full step. If you use a standard .010–.046 set, that low D string will lose significant tension, potentially causing fret buzz and a "flubby" attack under heavy picking.

**Expert Recommendation on String Gauge:**
Instead of a straight .010 or .011 set, I highly recommend a **"Skinny Top / Heavy Bottom" .010–.052 set**.

* The .010–.017 plain strings will feel normal in Eb Standard and comfortable for bends.
* The .030–.052 wound strings provide the necessary physical resistance and magnetic mass when dropped to D or C# (if you ever venture into Drop C#). It keeps your rhythm tracking tight and percussive.
* *Note:* Moving to a .052 low string will likely require a slight widening of the nut slot to prevent binding, and a minor truss rod adjustment to compensate for the altered neck relief.

Your Matt Heafy 7-string could technically cover lower registers, but mapping DADGAD voicings onto a 7-string neck is counterintuitive and unnecessarily complicates the fretboard geometry. The modified Schecter is the correct tool for this job.

When calibrating a guitar for specific tunings like DADGAD or Eb Standard, string tension dictates both playability and harmonic intonation. Here is the technical breakdown of the gauge sets you are considering for the Schecter.

| String Set | Typical Gauges | Pros | Cons | Best Application |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **.010 (Regular Light)** | .010 - .046 | Excellent balance of lead bendability and chordal tension. | Low strings become flubby and lose pitch stability in drop tunings. | E Standard or Eb Standard on a 25.5" scale. |
| **.011 (Medium)** | .011 - .048/49 | High tension ensures tight rhythm tracking and strong acoustic coupling. | Requires significantly more hand and grip strength for wide vibrato and bends. | D Standard or C Standard setups. |

Forcing a heavier gauge into a narrow slot causes binding, which immediately ruins tuning stability. You will also need to adjust the truss rod to compensate for the altered neck relief.
