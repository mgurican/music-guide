## 1. Tremolo Picking

In the context of your repertoire—specifically **Rhapsody**, **Iron Maiden**, and **Dick Dale-style surf influences** often found in Satriani’s work—tremolo picking is the rapid, continuous repetition of a single note using alternating strokes (down-up-down-up).

### The Mechanics

* **Action:** It is a high-frequency "shred" technique where the pick oscillates across the string with minimal depth.
**The Signal Chain Factor:** For a track like *Emerald Sword* (Rhapsody), you need a fast tracking response.

* **The Gear Fix:** Use your **Schecter Reaper with the Crunchlab**. The Crunchlab’s high output provides the necessary compression to keep every stroke even.
* **Pedal Tip:** Engage the **TC Electronic Spark Mini** as a clean boost before your **JVM410c** (Orange or Red mode). The added gain will "smooth out" the gaps between pick strokes, making the tremolo sound like a continuous wall of sound rather than individual clicks.

## 2. Natural Harmonics

Natural harmonics are "overtones" produced by lightly touching a string at specific mathematical divisions (nodes) of its length without pressing it down to the fret.

### The Physics

When you pluck a string normally, it vibrates at its fundamental frequency. When you place a finger lightly over a fret wire (like the 5th, 7th, or 12th) and pluck, you cancel out the fundamental frequency and allow the higher overtones to ring through.

### Critical Nodes for Your Style:

* **12th Fret:** Octave above the open string. (Classic **Rush** / Alex Lifeson textures).
* **7th Fret:** Perfect 5th above the octave.
* **5th Fret:** Two octaves above the open string.
* **The "Tool" Factor:** Adam Jones frequently uses natural harmonics at the **3rd fret** and even the **2nd fret** (which are much harder to ring out).

### Optimizing Your Tone for Harmonics:

* **Pickup Selection:** Use the **Bridge pickup** (Crunchlab or the Majesty’s DiMarzio). Bridge pickups emphasize the upper-mid frequencies where harmonics live.
**Clipping & Compression:** Natural harmonics have a lower output than fretted notes.

* **The Pro Move:** Engage your **Boss SD-1**. Because the SD-1 uses **asymmetrical clipping**, it generates more even-order harmonics. This "sweetens" the sound and helps those fragile 5th and 3rd fret natural harmonics sustain longer and cut through the mix.
* **Amp Setting:** On your **Mesa Mark VII**, ensure the "Bright" switch is engaged on the Lead channel to help those high-frequency nodes "pop."
