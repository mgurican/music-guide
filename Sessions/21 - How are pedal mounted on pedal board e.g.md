Regarding your question on pedal mounting—specifically for rail-style aluminum boards like the Harley Benton Spaceship series—you have a few distinct methods to secure your gear. Given the quality of your pedals, stability and maintaining a clean signal path are the priorities.
Here are the primary mounting methods used by touring professionals and techs, along with their pros and cons:
**1. 3M Dual Lock (The Professional Standard)**
Instead of fabric loops, Dual Lock uses interlocking plastic mushroom-shaped stems.

* **Pros:** Incredible rigidity. Pedals will not sag or wobble when you engage switches. It requires significantly less surface area than standard Velcro to achieve a secure hold.
* **Cons:** The adhesive backing is aggressively strong. **Pro Tip:** Apply a layer of blue painter's tape to the back of your vintage or valuable pedals (like your TS808) before applying Dual Lock. This prevents the adhesive from ripping off the original manufacturer stickers or paint if you ever decide to sell them.

**2. Standard Hook and Loop (Velcro)**
This is the material that usually ships in the box with Harley Benton boards. You apply the "loop" (soft side) to the board's rails and the "hook" (scratchy side) to the pedals.

* **Pros:** Easily accessible, cheap, and makes swapping pedals in and out of your chain very fast.
* **Cons:** Pedals can have a slight "squish" or wobble when stepped on. The adhesive can degrade and melt under hot stage lights, and the loops attract dust and pet hair.

**3. Zip Ties (The Old-School Tech Route)**
Utilizing the gaps between the rails on a Harley Benton board to strap the pedals directly to the metal.

* **Pros:** Completely eliminates the need for adhesive on your pedals. Extremely secure.
* **Cons:** Can look cluttered if not done meticulously. Swapping a pedal out requires wire cutters and rebuilding that section of your signal chain.

**Best Practices for a Harley Benton Board Setup:**

* **Surface Prep:** Before applying any adhesive (Velcro or Dual Lock) to the aluminum rails, wipe them down thoroughly with isopropyl alcohol. This strips away manufacturing oils and ensures a permanent bond.
* **Cable Routing:** Use the gaps between the rails to pass your power and patch cables underneath the deck. Mount an isolated power supply under the board to keep power lines away from your audio signal path, reducing electromagnetic interference (EMI) and high-gain hiss—crucial when you are running a Mark VII or JVM.
