> **what about wiring? what are options? currently there is push/pull on both controls, how to wire these?**

#### Gem Response
Transitioning from the **Fishman Fluence** system to a **Passive Pegasus/Sentient** set in a **Matt Heafy Origins 7** requires a complete gutting of the active circuitry. Since your guitar has a 4-control layout (2 Volume, 2 Tone) and you want to utilize push/pull functionality, you have several sophisticated wiring options that rival the versatility of your **Majesty**.

### The Fundamental Requirement: 500k Pots

You must replace all four 25k pots with **500k Long-Shaft Push/Pull pots**. Using the existing 25k pots with passive pickups will result in a muffled, unusable tone (the "blanket over the amp" effect).

### Option 1: The "Modern Standard" (Individual Coil Splits)

This is the most direct translation of your current setup. It treats each pickup independently, similar to how many **Dream Theater** or **Satriani** tones are achieved.

* **Neck Volume (Push/Pull):** Splits the **Sentient** to a single coil.
* **Bridge Volume (Push/Pull):** Splits the **Pegasus** to a single coil.
* **Tones:** Standard master tone for each.
* **Sonic Result:** Gives you 8 distinct tonal combinations. Ideal for **Rush** or **Alice Cooper** cleans where you need that "glassy" strat-like chime.

### Option 2: The "Petrucci/Majesty" Style (Inner Coils)

Since you own a **Majesty**, you know the magic of the middle position with inner coils split. You can replicate this on the Heafy using a 3-way toggle and one push/pull.

* **Bridge Volume (Push/Pull):** When pulled, it splits **both** pickups simultaneously.
* **The Magic:** In the middle toggle position, you get the two inner coils in parallel. This produces that "acoustic-like" Sparkle—perfect for **Rhapsody**'s neo-classical clean passages or **Tool**'s ambient sections.

### Option 3: Series/Parallel (The "Audiophile" Choice)

Instead of a standard coil split (which cuts half the output and can add hum), you wire the humbuckers for **Parallel** mode.

* **Function:** Pulling the pot switches the humbucker coils from Series to Parallel.
* **Sonic Result:** You keep the hum-canceling properties, but the output drops and the treble increases. It sounds like a "hi-fi" single coil.
* **Why for you?** Given your interest in **Rachmaninov** and **Dvorak**, the Parallel Neck setting provides a piano-like clarity that is more sophisticated than a standard coil split.

### Technical Implementation: The "Pegasus" Color Code

Seymour Duncan uses a specific color code that differs from your Schecter (DiMarzio). To wire these to a push/pull for a standard split:

* **Black:** Hot (to the lug on the Volume pot).
* **Red & White:** Tied together and soldered to the center lug of the Push/Pull switch.
* **Green & Bare:** Soldered to the ground (back of the pot).

### Necessary Components for the Swap

To do this right, you’ll need more than just the pickups:

* **(4) 500k Ohm Long-Shaft Push/Pull Pots** (CTS or Bourns are recommended for your JVM/Mesa rig).
* **(1) .022µF Capacitor** (Orange Drop is the industry standard for that **Iron Maiden/Priest** bite).
* **(1) Stereo to Mono Jack Conversion:** You can keep the stereo jack, but you must re-wire it for a mono signal (Active jacks have a third "battery" lug that you will now ignore).

**Pro-Tip:** Since you are a **Tool** fan, consider wiring the Bridge Tone pot as a "No-Load" pot. When set to 10, it completely removes itself from the circuit, giving the **Pegasus** an extra 5% of raw, aggressive bite that matches Adam Jones' silverburst roar.
