## Petrucci's Compressor Setup

John Petrucci currently relies on the **TC Electronic HyperGravity Compressor**. He worked directly with TC Electronic to develop two specific TonePrints for it: "TransComp" and "EverComp". Historically, he has also used the Carl Martin Compressor Limiter, and occasionally a Darkglass Hyper Luminal for specific clean applications.

### How and Where He Uses It

Petrucci does not use compression on his heavy rhythm channels—the high-gain Mesa channels naturally compress the signal. Instead, he uses it strictly for:

* **Pristine Cleans:** He uses compression to add sustain, round off the harsh transient peaks of his piezo or split-coil magnetic pickups, and keep arpeggiated chords completely even.
* **Key Songs:** You will hear this heavily applied on the clean intros of "Pull Me Under," the funky, percussive clean sections of "Take the Time," and the atmospheric passages in "Barstool Warrior."
* **Liquid Leads:** For incredibly dense, fast alternate picking passages (like the blistering unison solo in "In the Name of God"), heavy compression helps ensure every single 16th note jumps out with the exact same volume and intensity.

## Optimizing Your Signal Chain

To get the most out of your pedals and amps, your routing needs to exploit the different clipping mechanisms of your drives.

### Front of the Amp (Direct to Amp Input)

* **TC Electronic Spark Mini:** Place this first. It is a clean boost (up to 20dB). Hitting the front of your drives or your amp with this pushes the tubes harder without altering your EQ curve. It essentially functions just like the built-in 20dB push-push boost on your Majesty's volume knob.
* **Ibanez TS808 (Symmetrical Clipping):** This pedal smooths the signal and introduces a massive mid-hump while cutting flubby low-end frequencies. It is the industry standard for tightening up high-gain American amps.
* **Boss SD-1 (Asymmetrical Clipping):** Asymmetrical clipping introduces even-order harmonics, resulting in a rawer, edgier, and more aggressive bite. It retains more of the guitar's natural grit compared to a Tube Screamer.

### The FX Loop

Run these between the amp's Preamp Out and Power Amp In. You never want to distort time-based effects.

* **TC Electronic Dreamscape** -> **Marshall Delay** -> **TC Triple Delay**

## Tone Calibration: Mesa vs. Marshall

Here is how to dial in your two amplifiers using your existing pedalboard to match your core influences.

### The Dream Theater Tone (Mesa Mark VII)

The Mark series is defined by placing the graphic EQ *after* the preamp stage. The key to Petrucci's tone is keeping the preamp bass extremely low to prevent mud, and shaping the massive low-end using the graphic EQ later.

* **Channel:** Mark IV or IIC+ Mode
* **Gain:** 7
* **Bass:** 3
* **Mids:** 6
* **Treble:** 6.5
* **Graphic EQ (The Classic "V"):** Cut the 750Hz slider deeply to remove boxy midrange. Boost the 80Hz slider for massive sub-thump, and boost the 2200Hz and 6600Hz sliders for presence and pick attack.
* **Drive Strategy:** Engage the **TS808** (Drive: 0, Tone: 6, Level: 10). The symmetrical clipping tightens the Mark VII's low-end response for precise, percussive galloping.

### The Iron Maiden / Judas Priest Tone (Marshall JVM410c)

British heavy metal relies on power-amp roar and an aggressive upper-midrange snarl rather than scooped sub-bass.

* **Channel:** OD1 Orange
* **Gain:** 5 (The JVM has an enormous amount of gain; keep it lower to maintain string articulation).
* **Bass:** 5
* **Mids:** 7 to 8 (Do not scoop the mids on a Marshall—this is where the guitar lives in a dense mix).
* **Treble:** 6
* **Drive Strategy:** Engage the **Boss SD-1** (Drive: 2, Tone: 5, Level: 8). The asymmetrical clipping of the SD-1 pushes the Marshall's front end perfectly, giving you that biting, harmonically rich crunch essential for "The Trooper" or "Painkiller."
