> **how EVH D-tuna works and whart it does?**

#### Gem Response
The EVH D-Tuna is a brilliant, purely mechanical piece of hardware designed to solve the exact tuning dilemma you are facing with your Floyd Rose.
Here is the technical breakdown of its function and mechanics.

### What It Does

* **Instant Pitch Shifting:** It allows you to drop the pitch of your lowest string exactly one whole step (e.g., from Standard E down to Drop D, or in your case, from Eb down to Drop C#) in a fraction of a second.
* **Nut Bypass:** It executes this pitch drop entirely at the bridge. You do not need to unlock the locking nut, detune the tuning peg, and relock the nut.

### How It Works (The Mechanics)

The D-Tuna replaces the standard, long string-locking insert screw on the 6th string saddle of your Floyd Rose. It consists of a spring-loaded, sliding cylinder.

* **Pushed In (Standard Pitch - Eb):** When you slide the cylinder inward toward the neck, it wedges itself under the fine-tuner arm of the bridge. Acting as a mechanical spacer, it physically forces the saddle to tilt backward, tightening the string to your higher pitch (Eb).
* **Pulled Out (Drop Pitch - C#):** When you pull the cylinder outward, the spacer is removed from under the fine-tuner arm. The string's native tension pulls the saddle slightly forward, dropping the pitch precisely one whole step (C#).
* **Dual Calibration:** The system uses two independent fine-tuning points. You tune your standard pitch (Eb) using the regular fine-tuner on the Floyd Rose baseplate. You tune the dropped pitch (C#) using a micro Allen set-screw embedded directly into the side of the D-Tuna cylinder, which dictates exactly how far the saddle tilts forward when disengaged.

### The Critical Constraint: "Dive-Only" Operation

As previously established, dropping the tension of the 6th string removes overall forward pull on the bridge.

* **The Rule:** The D-Tuna **will not work** on a fully floating Floyd Rose. If your bridge is floating, pulling the D-Tuna out will cause the bridge to sink backward, sending your other five strings violently sharp.
* **The Fix:** To use a D-Tuna, your Floyd Rose must be blocked from pulling up. It must rest flush against the wood of the guitar body cavity, or be physically stopped by an internal brass block or a device like a Tremol-No. This ensures the bridge baseplate cannot move backward when the 6th string tension drops, keeping your high strings perfectly in tune.

* **The Plus:** It costs nothing. Furthermore, wedging dense wood directly against the tremolo block creates a direct mechanical coupling to the guitar body, which noticeably increases your acoustic resonance and sustain.
