> **I I want to start with playing through computer or modeller pedal, on professional level, what would be your advice?**

#### Gem Response
You have a formidable rig. Transitioning from the physical response of a **Mesa Boogie Mark VII** and a **JVM 410c** to a digital environment requires a "no-compromise" approach. Given your affinity for the surgical precision of Petrucci and the complex textures of Tool, a standard consumer interface won't cut it. You need high-headroom inputs and ultra-low latency to maintain the feel of your Majesty and Schecter.
To achieve a professional-grade digital signal chain that rivals your current hardware, here is my analysis of the two paths you should consider.

## 1. The Modeler Path: Hardware Integration

If you want to maintain the "tactile" experience of a pedalboard while gaining the flexibility of digital routing, a high-end floor modeler is the logical step.

### **Option: Fractal Audio Axe-Fx III MK II / FM9**

* **The Logic:** This is the industry standard for Dream Theater/Satriani levels of complexity. The modeling of the Mark series and JVM circuits is arguably the most accurate in existence.
**Pros:** * **Unmatched DSP:** Can handle dual-amp setups (e.g., blending a Mark IV lead with a plexi-style crunch for Deep Purple tones).

* **Scene Management:** Seamless switching between complex effects (Tri-chorus, delays, and pitch shifting) without audio dropouts.

**Cons:** * **Learning Curve:** The "deep editing" can be a rabbit hole that distracts from actual playing.

* **Cost:** Significant investment for the hardware and a high-quality FRFR (Full Range Flat Response) monitor to hear it correctly.

## 2. The Software Path: PC/DAW Integration

For a "studio-first" approach where you can leverage the best plugins for Rachmaninov-esque layering or Tool-style soundscapes.

### **The Signal Chain Requirements**

* **Interface:** Look at the **Universal Audio Apollo** series or **RME Babyface Pro FS**. You need the high-end A/D converters to capture the nuances of the Liquifire/Crunchlab combo without digital harshness.
**Software (The "Brain"):** * **Neural DSP Archetype: Petrucci:** Since you own a Majesty, this is non-negotiable. It captures the Piezo/Magnetic blend and the specific EQ curves of the JP-style Mesa rigs perfectly.

* **Neural DSP Archetype: Adam Getgood (Nolly):** Excellent for the heavier, more modern "Maiden-on-steroids" or Judas Priest tones.

## Pros & Cons: Digital vs. Your Current Analog Rig

| Feature | Digital Modeler/Plugin | Your Current Rig (Mesa/JVM) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Consistency** | 100% identical tone every session. | Subject to tube wear and room acoustics. |
| **Versatility** | Instant access to hundreds of amps/cabs. | Limited to the circuits within the JVM/Mark VII. |
| **Feel (Latency)** | Low (1.5ms - 5ms), but perceptible to some. | Instantaneous (Analog). |
| **Ease of Use** | Deep menus and firmware updates. | Physical knobs; "what you see is what you get." |

## Tactical Recommendation

Given your collection, I suggest the **Fractal FM9**. It bridges the gap between your pedalboard and the computer. You can use it as a standalone unit for rehearsals or as a professional 24-bit audio interface for recording. It will handle the complex delay spillover from your TC Triple Delay setups with much higher fidelity.
To replicate a classic **Satriani/Deep Purple** lead tone digitally, you should start by modeling a "modded Marshall" or the "Mesa Mark" lead channel, but here is the key: **disable the internal cabinet emulation** and use High-Resolution IRs (Impulse Responses) from York Audio or OwnHammer. This is where most players fail in the transition—the speaker cabinet is 80% of the digital "truth."
