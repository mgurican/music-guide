Given the high-end nature of your current rig (Mark VII, JVM), you need the electronics to be spot-on to avoid high-end roll-off.
The **Schecter Diamond Decimator** pickups are **passive, high-output humbuckers**.
Here are the correct specifications for their potentiometer pairing:

### **The Specs**

**Resistance:** **500k Ohm**

* **Why:** These are passive humbuckers. Using 250k (standard for single coils) would bleed off too much treble, making these already dark/modern pickups sound muddy.

**Taper:**

* **Volume:** **Audio Taper (Logarithmic)** is standard. This allows for a smooth swell and cleans up the gain signal effectively when you roll back—vital for dynamic playing on your Mesa Mark VII.
* **Tone:** **Linear Taper** is often found on stock Schecters for the tone pot, though Audio Taper works fine if you prefer a quick drop-off.

* **Capacitor:** **.022µF** (Standard for humbuckers).

### **Sonic Characteristics vs. Your Current Set**

Since you currently run the **DiMarzio Liquifire/Crunch Lab** set in your Reaper, here is what you can expect if you swap back to the Decimators:

* **Output:** The Decimators are very hot—likely hotter than the Crunch Lab. They are designed to push the front end of an amp hard.
* **EQ Profile:** They are more "scooped" and modern-sounding than the Crunch Lab. The Crunch Lab has a very focused, tight mid-range (that "Petrucci" vocal quality). The Decimators have a sizzling high-end and a tighter, but less "musical" low-end.
* **Clarity:** The Liquifire (neck) is legendary for fluid, flute-like lead tones. The Decimator neck pickup will feel stiffer and a bit more sterile in comparison.

### **Expert Tip on Wiring**

The Schecter Reaper usually features a **Push-Pull Tone Pot** for coil-splitting. If you are wiring these back in, ensure your push-pull pot is also **500k**. If you use a standard 250k pot on a high-output ceramic pickup like the Decimator, you will lose that "cutting" frequency that helps you sit in a mix with keys (Dream Theater style).

### 4. [Feb 15, 2026, 5:33:48 PM CEST] Prompt
> **Are those pots good for crunchlab/liquifire combo while upgrade?**

#### Gem Response
The short answer is **yes, they are electrically compatible**, but they are the "weak link" in terms of mechanical feel and tolerance consistency for a rig of your caliber.
Here is the honest analysis of using stock Schecter pots with your high-end DiMarzio set:

### The Pluses (Why it works)

* **Correct Resistance (500k):** The Crunch Lab and LiquiFire are naturally warm, mid-heavy pickups. The stock 500k pots are essential to retain high-end cut. If you were to drop to 250k (common in some strats), your Mark VII would sound like it has a blanket over the cab.
* **Split Capability:** The stock push-pull tone pot allows you to split the coils. This is critical for your musical tastes—specifically for the **Rush** (Alex Lifeson) clean passages or the "glassy" clean tones Petrucci uses (center position, inner coils active).

### The Minuses (Why you might upgrade)

* **Taper & Roll-off:** Stock Alpha pots often have a rapid drop-off in volume between 10 and 8. With your **JVM 410c** or **Mark VII**, you likely rely on the volume knob to clean up the signal for dynamic passages (Deep Purple style). Stock pots struggle to provide a smooth, linear cleanup; it’s often "all or nothing."
* **Tolerance:** Generic pots can vary by +/- 20%. If your volume pot is actually reading 450k instead of 500k, you are losing treble bite before the signal even hits your pedalboard.
* **Friction Feel:** Your **Music Man Majesty** likely uses custom DiMarzio or high-end CTS pots with very low friction for rapid swells. Going from the Majesty to the stock Reaper pots will feel "stiff" or "scratchy," affecting your ability to perform smooth volume swells (a la **Satriani** or **Rhapsody** intros).

### The Verdict

If the pickups are already installed and working, leave the pots alone until they scratch. However, if you are currently soldering or planning to refine the guitar, I strongly recommend swapping the Volume pot for a **DiMarzio Custom Taper** or a **Bourns Low Friction 500k**.

* **Why?** It will match the feel of your Majesty and give you precise control over the gain stages of your Mesa and Marshall amps.
